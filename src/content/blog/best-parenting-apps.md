Every new parent is handed the same advice: *there's an app for that*. What nobody tells you is that most parenting apps are built for the wrong moment. They assume you have two free hands, a full night's sleep, and an appetite for filling in forms. At 3am, with a baby on one arm, you have none of those.

We spend our days building software, and one of the things we build is a baby tracker called **Wobble**. So take the ranking below for what it is — our opinion, from people with a horse in the race. We have tried to be straight about what the other apps do well, because a list that pretends the competition is bad is not worth reading.

Here are the five parenting apps we think are worth your attention this year.

---

## 1. Wobble — the best all-round baby tracker

**Best for:** milestones, growth, and daily logs for a child's first three years, without handing your data to anyone.

Wobble covers the whole first-three-years story in one place: milestones, feeds, naps, diapers, teeth, first foods, vaccines, and growth charts. But the reason we put it first is not the feature list — it is three decisions that most trackers get wrong.

**It never grades your baby.** Milestones are checked off against gentle age *ranges*, not deadlines. And if your baby arrived early, Wobble uses adjusted age automatically, the way your pediatrician does — so a preemie is never measured against a due date they never had. This sounds like a small thing until you have lain awake wondering why the app says your child is "behind."

**Your baby's data never leaves your phone.** No account. No sign-up. No cloud. Wobble stores everything locally, which means there is no server holding a timeline of your child's weight, sleep and photos. The free version shows one small banner ad, and the ad system never sees your child's data.

**It was designed for the middle of the night.** Feeds, naps and diapers are two taps. There is a warm dark mode, and reminders respect quiet hours. Growth measurements land on percentile charts with plain-language guidance — because every curve is normal when it follows its own line.

Wobble is free and complete for one or two children, with photo memories and shareable milestone cards. Wobble Plus, an optional subscription, adds room for up to five children, encrypted backup, restore-to-a-new-phone, sharing with a partner, and no ads. Plus adds to the free app; it never takes anything away from it.

**The honest caveat:** Wobble is Android-only today — an iOS version is in the works but not out yet. And because there is no cloud by default, moving to a new phone means using the Plus backup. That is the deliberate trade-off of keeping everything local, and it is the one thing you should weigh before choosing it.

## 2. Huckleberry — the best for sleep

**Best for:** parents whose main problem is naps.

If your question is "when should the next nap be?", Huckleberry is the app built around answering it. It logs sleep, feeds and diapers like any tracker, but its distinguishing feature is nap-timing prediction based on your child's own logged patterns, plus sleep guidance layered on top.

**Where it falls short:** it is narrower than it looks. Sleep is the centre of gravity, and the broader first-three-years picture — teeth, first foods, vaccine records, milestone memories — is not really its job. Its more advanced guidance sits behind a subscription, and it works from an account rather than staying on-device.

## 3. BabyCenter — the best for week-by-week reading

**Best for:** parents who want to *read* more than they want to log.

BabyCenter has been a fixture of pregnancy and early parenthood for decades, and its strength is editorial: week-by-week development articles, an enormous library on everything from teething to tantrums, and active community boards where you can find other parents at exactly your stage.

**Where it falls short:** it is a content app with tracking bolted on, not the reverse. If what you want is a fast, quiet log of today's feeds, there is a lot of magazine in the way — and a lot of advertising. Community boards are a genuine comfort at 3am and also, occasionally, a source of anxiety.

## 4. The Wonder Weeks — the best for understanding the fussy weeks

**Best for:** making sense of the sudden, inexplicable bad fortnight.

Built on the well-known book, The Wonder Weeks maps your baby's mental development "leaps" against their age and tells you when to expect the clingy, sleepless stretches. For a lot of parents its real value is emotional: knowing that this week has a name and an end date.

**Where it falls short:** it does one thing. It is not a tracker, and the leap-based model is a popular framework rather than settled science — useful as reassurance, less useful as a health record. Most parents run it alongside another app rather than instead of one.

## 5. Baby Tracker — the best no-frills logger

**Best for:** people who want a log and nothing else.

Baby Tracker (from Nighp Software) has stayed popular for a simple reason: it logs feeds, sleep, diapers and pumping quickly and gets out of the way. Reports and charts are straightforward, and it is easy to hand to a partner or a grandparent without explaining anything.

**Where it falls short:** the interface shows its age, and the scope stops at daily logging. Milestones, growth percentiles, teeth, foods and vaccines are somebody else's problem. It is a good tool with a small job.

## How to choose

| If your priority is… | Start with |
| --- | --- |
| The whole first three years in one place | **Wobble** |
| Keeping your child's data off the cloud | **Wobble** |
| Cracking a nap schedule | Huckleberry |
| Reading and community | BabyCenter |
| Understanding fussy phases | The Wonder Weeks |
| The quickest possible daily log | Baby Tracker |

The genuinely useful test is not which app has the most features. It is whether you will still be using it in four months. An app you can operate one-handed, half-asleep, without logging in, is an app that survives the newborn phase. That is the standard we built Wobble to meet, and it is why it sits at the top of this list.

One last thing worth saying plainly: none of these apps — Wobble included — is medical advice. A tracker is there to help you notice and remember. Your pediatrician is there to interpret. Keep those jobs separate and every app on this list gets more useful.
